Lesson four

Say her name.

📚
HERMIONE

Her-my-oh-nee. Listen to the very start of it. That sound — /er/ — is the single most common sound in English writing.

It also has five different spellings, and you cannot hear which one you need. That is not you being bad at spelling. That is English being a mess.

Same sound. Every time.

Say these five out loud

Listen to the gold bit in each one. It's the identical sound in all five.

One sound. Five spellings. er, ir, ur, ear, or.

There's no rule that tells you which. So you don't learn a rule — you learn five names, and you sort every new word under one of them.

Five anchors

Tap each one

Each name holds a spelling, and a gang of ordinary words that use it too.

The only rule in the lesson

Wormtail's rule

🐀
WORMTAIL

After a w, the /er/ sound is nearly always spelled or.

Which fixes a whole pile of words in one go:

word   work   world
worm   worth   worse

Six words you probably guess at, sorted by one rat.

Your turn

Which spelling?

Every one of these has lost its /er/. Put the right spelling back.

📚
Last bit

Which anchor do you want to draw?

Draw it, then write its name inside the picture with the /er/ spelling much bigger than the rest of the letters.

🏆

Lesson four, done.

Five spellings, five anchors, and one rat with a rule.

Your five

📚 er Hermione  ·  🧹 ir Firebolt  ·  🏰 ur Durmstrang
🍬 ear Earwax  ·  🐀 or Wormtail

When you get stuck

Don't sound it out — it won't help, there's nothing to hear. Ask instead: which name does this word belong to?
