Lesson six

Words change shape when you add to them.

🌙
STUNNED

Stun has one N. Stunned has two. Nobody added that second N for decoration — something made it happen, and the same thing happens to hundreds of other words.

Three rules. That's the whole lesson. Learn them and you can transfigure almost any word that takes an ending.

Three transfigurations

Watch what changes

Each one takes a plain word and adds an ending — but the word has to change first.

Double it. Drop the e. Turn Y into I. Three rules, and they cover most of the words that catch people out.

Three rules

Tap each one

Each rule tells you what to do to the base word before you add the ending.

Lesson six, done.

Three rules. Hundreds of words. That is a very good trade.

Your three rules

⚡ Double it — short vowel + one consonant → stunned
🪶 Drop the e — word ends in silent e → levitating
🗿 Y → I — consonant before the y → petrified

When you're stuck

Look at the end of the base word. Short vowel then one consonant? Double it. Silent e? Drop it. Consonant then y? Y becomes i.

Tutor — ideas for the rest of the session

The lesson above is the input. Pick one or two of these for what's left of the hour — you do not need to do all of them, and you do not need to do the same ones every week.

🔍 Word espionage  10 min
Take a page of whatever Sofia is reading — or better, a page of her own writing — and hunt for the pattern from this lesson. Highlight what you find. Use the standard colours so the three jobs stay visible: green for prefixes, yellow for roots, blue for suffixes. This is the step that carries the learning out of the session and into her schoolwork.
🧠 Word mastery questions  5 min
Pick any word from the lesson and ask:
• How many pieces does it have? What are they?
• What does each piece do or mean?
• Did the root change when the ending went on?
• Can you swap the root and keep the ending?
• Can you think of another word with this piece in it?

Sofia should be doing most of the talking. If she gets one wrong, ask the next question rather than correcting her — she will usually get there.
✨ Invent a word  5 min
Make up a word that does not exist, using real pieces, and work out together what it would mean. Spectable. Immortion. Subdictive. Then let her invent one for you and see if you can read it.

This is not a game to fill time — being able to decode a word you have never seen is the entire point of learning the pieces.
🗣️ Teach it back  5 min
Sofia explains this lesson's pattern to you as if you have never heard of it. You are allowed to play dumb and ask awkward questions. If she can teach it, she has it — and if she stalls at a particular spot, that is exactly the bit to revisit next session.
